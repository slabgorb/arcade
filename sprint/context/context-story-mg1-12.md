## Problem
CLAUDE.md records the gap in its own words: 'Task 1 measured which buckets and domains exist; NOTHING has measured what any of them serves', and closes with the rule this story exists to honour — 'do not infer a live game from a live hostname; request it.' The seven arcade-<game> buckets still exist and their <game>.slabgorb.com custom domains are still bound to them; unbinding those for Single Redirects onto arcade.slabgorb.com/<id>/ is the owner's step and had not been taken. Before ANY of that is touched, and long before the irreversible teardown, somebody has to fetch each hostname and record what came back. Five of the seven (tempest, star-wars, asteroids, battlezone, red-baron) were the production cabinet before the migration and appeared in the retired hosting table, so they are expected-live. Joust was doubted and is cleared: arcade-joust was created 2026-07-26 and Task 1 released joust v0.0.8 with a green CI deploy — but note a green deploy proves an UPLOAD RAN, not what a hostname serves today, which is the whole point of this story. CENTIPEDE IS THE ONE GENUINELY UNVERIFIED CASE: it appeared in no hosting table and nothing on record shows a deploy to its bucket. THE BAR IS A LIVE 200 WITH A BODY, NOT A GREEN SUITE — fetch each index.html and at least one hashed asset per host, record the status, the content-type and enough of the body to tell a real build from a bucket-default or an error page. Record the result in docs/ops/hosting.md so the teardown has something to check itself against instead of an assumption.
